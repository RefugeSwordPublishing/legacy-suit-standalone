# GuildWright Growth Marketing — Research Round 1

_Last updated 2026-08-08. First research pass: competitive landscape, positioning, paid vs organic economics. No budget committed._

## TL;DR
- **Flat pricing is the wedge.** Per-user pricing is the single most-complained-about thing in contractor software. GuildWright's flat $29 (Field) / $99 (Pro) per company, whole crew included, is a quantifiable, defensible edge. Lead with it everywhere.
- **SEO before ads.** Bottom-of-funnel comparison/alternative pages drive 40–60% of SaaS conversions and are free/compounding. Paid search is getting expensive and should be a small, tracked test later.
- **Track first.** Wire GA4/Vercel Analytics + a lead-form conversion event before any spend.

## The competitive landscape (two clusters GuildWright spans)
**Time-tracking wedge (Field-tier competitors), all per-user + base fee:**
- ClockShark ~$9–11/user + $40 base; busybusy ~$9.99–14.99/user; Workyard ~$6–13/user + $50 base; ConstructionClock; OnTheClock; Timeero; Connecteam.

**All-in-one office / PM (Pro-tier competitors):**
- Buildertrend $499–799/mo; Contractor Foreman ~$49–199/mo; JobTread ~$199/mo; Knowify; Jobber $39+/mo (service trades, not project-based, no section estimates); Housecall Pro; CoConstruct; Projul (flat ~$399/mo, one of the few flat players).

**GuildWright is unusual in spanning BOTH** (field time-clock + office estimate/invoice/change orders) at a flat $29/$99. Most rivals are either a time tool OR an office tool.

## The killer differentiator: flat pricing (validated)
Documented contractor complaints about per-user pricing:
- "Growth tax" — the bill rises with every hire; teams share logins or keep people off the system to avoid fees.
- A ~50-person team on typical per-seat software = **$18,000+/year** before add-ons; bills "triple within six months."
- Rising interest in flat-rate alternatives (Projul, Contractor Foreman lean into this).

GuildWright is flat per company. Example math to put on the site/ads: **15 field workers on ClockShark ≈ $175/mo; on GuildWright Field, $29 flat.** Same story vs Workyard/busybusy. This is the headline.

## Paid search reality check (don't conflate two markets)
- **Contractor → homeowner** keywords ("kitchen remodel near me") run **$20–80 CPC**. NOT our market.
- **B2B software** keywords ("construction time tracking software", "contractor estimating software") are our market — a SaaS-competitive space where software vendors bid hard; expect meaningful CPCs and longer payback.
- Contractor-space CPCs are up **10–25% in 2026**; paid is pricier every year.
- Implication: ads are a **later, small-budget, tightly-tracked test**, not the first move. A $50 click at a 10% landing-page conversion = $500 per lead — only worth it with good tracking and a sharp landing page.

## SEO strategy (highest ROI — do first)
The SaaS SEO playbook maps cleanly to GuildWright:
1. **Comparison / alternative pages (bottom-of-funnel, 40–60% of SaaS conversions):**
   - "GuildWright vs ClockShark", "vs Buildertrend", "vs Contractor Foreman"
   - "ClockShark alternative (flat pricing)", "Buildertrend alternative for small contractors", "Jobber alternative for construction"
2. **Programmatic / template pages:** "free construction estimate template", "job costing spreadsheet", integration pages ("QuickBooks time tracking for construction", "Xero for contractors").
3. **Product-led how-to content** (reuse the help-center voice): "how to track crew hours", "how to bill schedule of values", "what are cost codes".

Organic = 40–60% of established SaaS traffic; compounding and free. This is the right first investment.

## Recommended sequence
1. **Analytics + conversion tracking** — GA4 / Vercel Analytics on guildwright.app + lead-form conversion event. Never spend before you can measure.
2. **SEO foundation + comparison/alternative pages** — flat-pricing wedge is the best organic weapon.
3. **Template/lead-magnet content** — free estimate template, etc.
4. **Small Google Ads test** — only after tracking + landing pages exist; bottom-funnel keywords; measure cost-per-signup; scale only if it pays.
